Words for voice AI that speaks Indian.

Written from Sarvam’s public product pages (sarvam.ai/models). No affiliation with Sarvam. Drafted with AI assistance; I chose the angle, structure and edited every line.

A · Thread for X / LinkedIn

Speech AI for code-mixed India

1“Kal meeting hai, please send the deck.” One sentence, three languages. For people in India that’s just talking. For a speech model, it’s a hard problem.
2Switching language mid-sentence is called code-mixing. Voice AI built for India has to handle it.
3Saaras V4 is Sarvam’s speech recognition model. It covers 22 Indian languages plus English, with output modes to transcribe, translate or codemix.
4Plain words: you speak the way you naturally speak, and it writes down or translates what you said.
5Want it to talk back? Bulbul V3 turns text into natural speech in 11 Indian languages.
6Listening and speaking the way people actually do. More at sarvam.ai.

Why this format: hook with a real sentence, explain in one plain line, end on one clear action.

B · Short LinkedIn post

What is a code-mixed sentence?

One that mixes languages in a single breath, like “Mujhe ye report tomorrow tak chahiye.” Many of us talk this way every day. Saaras V4 has a codemix output mode so the text matches how people really speak.

C · 30-second explainer script

Translate in one step

Visual: someone speaks a sentence in Hindi; English text appears on the phone.
Voiceover: You speak in your language. The AI listens. Saaras V4 can transcribe it, or translate it, across 22 Indian languages and English.
End card: Sarvam. Built for India.

Case studies

Four brands, read closely. Facts come from the sources under each brand. Analysis and recommendations are mine, developed with AI assistance, and are part of my Brand Teardown Series. Where I propose a test, I have no data yet; it is a hypothesis.

01 · Deep dive · Duolingo

When the voice that works for memes can’t carry layoffs

FactsDuolingo markets through its owl mascot, Duo, with a social-first, “unhinged” TikTok style. In April 2025 its CEO announced an “AI-first” approach that included gradually stopping use of contractors for work AI can do. It drew public backlash, and he later said he “did not give enough context”.
AnalysisThe character-led content works because it feels like a creator, not a corporate account. That same warmth raised the stakes: the audience that loves the owl is also the audience that cares about people versus AI. A joke voice and a jobs announcement send mixed signals.
Recommendation 1 · Frame sensitive news around what stays human For the next announcement about automation, lead with the roles and decisions that remain with people, then the change.
Test: two framings (company statement vs. a named team member explaining it). Measure: share of negative comments and mentions, and app-store rating in the following two weeks. Risk: reads as defensive if the human commitments are vague.
Recommendation 2 · Reduce mid-lesson upgrade prompts Protect the habit loop that the brand is built on.
Test: a holdout group seeing fewer prompts. Measure: day-7 retention against paid conversion. Risk: a short-term dip in subscription revenue, so judge it over a longer window.

Sources: The Verge · TechCrunch · The Drum

02 · Sugar Cosmetics

Sharp niche, costly stores

Facts: Founded 2015 by Vineeta Singh and Kaushik Mukherjee. FY25 (year ended 31 March 2025) revenue fell 17.8% to Rs 415 crore, per Storyboard18 citing The Arc. The brand is cutting its retail footprint.

Analysis: The Indian-skin-tone focus is its clearest difference. Offline expansion added cost without matching sales.

Do: keep the best stores, close the rest, move savings into quick commerce and founder-led content.

Storyboard18 · YourStory · Elle India

03 · Uniqlo

Product tech, cautious in India

Facts: “LifeWear” puts product technology (Heattech, AIRism) at the centre, with long-term ambassador deals such as Roger Federer since 2018. It has moved cautiously in India and is now speeding up store openings.

Analysis (my view): A basics-led range may feel safe to younger shoppers; this is a hypothesis to test with them, not a sourced finding.

Do: keep the core line, add a small rotating “quirky essentials” line tied to moments like exam season and festivals.

Uniqlo · Fast Retailing · BoF

04 · Bewakoof

Fun brand, quality gap

Facts: Social strategy built on humour and relatable memes. Partnered with Google Cloud for AI-generated T-shirt designs from text prompts. Some online reviews complain about wear after washes (anecdotal, not a measured rating).

Analysis: Perceived quality can limit what customers will pay.

Do: scheduled themed drops, plus a premium sub-line with better fabric and print.

Social Samosa · Gadgets360
